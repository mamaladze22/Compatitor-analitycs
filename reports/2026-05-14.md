## Per-advertiser notes

### Biblusi
Biblusi is running the most diversified ad set in the competitive set this week: 11 active ads across at least four distinct campaign types. The core of their spend is a May-long promotion stack — a 50%-off selected books offer (ad 1479129720606627, 9 days), a 1+1 deal on "Wignebi Batumshi" publisher titles (carousel ad 2041928350069514, 15 days, highest-run ad in their current set), and a 30% discount on Nonfiction series (ad 1454070896734068, "Send message" CTA, running May 11–17). The monthly catalog ad (ad 2290189301509157, linking to a Fliphtml5 digital catalog) is a notable format — they are using a paginated digital catalog as a landing experience rather than a product page.

The biggest development is the Litbox launch. Three ads went live May 13–14 (IDs 1526949895590515, 1298719928898742, 990396406878762), all Georgian-language, all linking to litbox.ge. One features celebrity endorsement from Mancho Nozadze as an early subscriber. The hook: "Litbox is a literary guide that combines a book with interesting, practical, and pleasant items." This is a subscription box product, a new revenue stream, and a loyalty mechanism. The launch is fresh and the run duration is only 1 day — it is too early to judge traction, but the multi-ad launch push suggests it is a real strategic initiative, not an experiment.

Also notable: a World Cup Panini sticker collection ad launched today (ad 2468119000289017), targeting football fans — a seasonal, non-book product category. Biblusi is clearly willing to merchandise adjacent categories.

This is a distribution model competitor publishers are not replicating.

Palitra L is also the only competitor confirmed running on Facebook Messenger as a primary placement (most of their ads are Facebook/Messenger only, not Instagram). This is an unusual placement choice worth noting.

## Patterns across the set

**Subscription and recurring-revenue models are the competitive frontier.** Palitra L has been building a subscription funnel for at least two years. Biblusi just launched a subscription box (Litbox) this week. No other competitor has this. Sulakauri has no subscription product visible in this scan.

**The 1+1 bundle is the most-used promotional mechanic.** Biblusi runs a 1+1 on Wignebi Batumshi titles; Palitra L ran many 1+1 carousels historically. It is a standard of the Georgian book market's promotional language.

**All copy is Georgian-language across the entire competitive set.** There is not a single English-primary ad in the scan. This validates that the Georgian-speaking parent/reader is the universal target and that English-language copy has no role in this market's paid acquisition.

**Scarcity and urgency are underused across the set** — Intelekti's 5-hour flash sale is the only pure urgency play. Biblusi uses "month-end" framing but that is a soft deadline. There is an opening for more aggressive scarcity mechanics.

**Format diversity is low.** Most ads are single-image or format-unknown (likely single image with copy). Biblusi uses carousels for the 1+1 promotion. Nobody is running video creatives in current active ads (Palitra L's subscription explainer video is the exception and it has 57 days of proof).

## What to study

**Ad 688406637668355 (Palitra L)** — The subscription explainer video, running 57 days on Facebook only. This is the most durable creative in the competitive set. It teaches the audience a process (how to sign up) rather than making a discount offer. The hook is educational, not promotional. If it has run 57 days it is converting. Worth pulling apart: what does the video show, what is the sign-up friction it overcomes, and why does Facebook-only placement work for this message when Instagram is absent.

**Ad 2041928350069514 (Biblusi)** — The 1+1 carousel for Wignebi Batumshi publisher titles, active for 15 days, running across Facebook/Instagram/Audience Network/WhatsApp/Threads. It is the longest-running Biblusi creative in this scan, deployed across the widest platform set. The creative has multiple images (at least 3 frames visible in the data). The headline framing — "for Biblusi's loyal readers" — is a social proof/loyalty hook rather than a raw discount hook. 15 days is enough to call this a winner in Biblusi's portfolio.

## Open questions

- **Litbox economics**: The Litbox launch is 1 day old. Is this a soft launch or a full push? Worth monitoring over the next 7–14 days to see if the ads scale or go dark. If they scale, Biblusi has a subscription product that directly competes with Palitra L.

- **Artanuji's status**: Are they entirely off Meta, running only organically, or shifted to another channel (Google, TikTok)? The complete absence of active ads is unusual for a publisher that was running campaigns as recently as January 2024.

- **Palitra L's distributor ads**: Three ads recruiting "book distributors" have run since October 2025 (218 days). What is the actual distributor network model — are these MLM-style resellers or retail channel partners? If it is working at 218-day duration, it represents a distribution moat.

- **Intelekti's flash sale result**: The May 15 flash sale (15:00–20:00) will have concluded. Did they run a follow-up ad? What was the volume impact? This would be worth manually checking their page or website on May 16.

- **Diogene's platform mix**: Their active ads are Facebook and Instagram only, no Audience Network or WhatsApp. Is this intentional targeting or budget constraint? Their literary-hook copy may skew toward older Facebook demographics rather than Instagram.
